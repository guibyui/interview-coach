# Behavioral Questions (STAR)

## Expected shape of a strong answer
**Situation, Task, Action, Result**, in about two minutes.
- **Situation and Task:** brief context and what was at stake. Do not spend half the answer here.
- **Action:** what *they* did (say "I", not "we"), and **why**, including alternatives they considered.
- **Result:** concrete and, where possible, quantified, plus the **trade-off accepted** or what they learned. An action without a result is an intention, not proof.

Always prefer one specific story over a general philosophy ("I usually mentor by..."). If the candidate answers in general terms, ask for one specific person and one specific moment.

Never invent a story for the candidate. If they have no real example, help them mine their projects for one (ask about outages, disagreements, changed requirements, onboarding someone, a hard deadline). If you give an example for illustration, label it clearly as a sample and ask them to replace it with something true.

## Questions

### Technical decision-making
1. Tell me about an architectural decision you made. What was the situation, what did you decide, and what trade-off did you accept?
2. Tell me about a time you chose a simpler solution over a more scalable one, or the reverse. How did you decide?
3. Tell me about a time you had to change direction on a technical decision after realizing it was not working.
4. Describe a system you built from scratch. What are you most proud of, and what would you do differently?
5. Tell me about a time you balanced moving fast against building something correct and scalable. Where did you draw the line?
6. Tell me about a major refactor or rebuild you led. How did you reduce risk and show it was worth it?

### Collaboration and conflict
7. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate or stakeholder on a technical approach. How did you handle it, and what was the outcome?
8. Tell me about a time requirements changed in the middle of a project.
9. Tell me about a time you had to explain a technical limitation or delay to a non-technical stakeholder.
10. How do you handle feedback in code review, as author and as reviewer?

### Leadership and mentoring
11. Tell me about a time you mentored someone less experienced. What was the actual teaching moment, and how do you know it stuck?
12. How do you review a junior engineer's code in a way that builds their judgment instead of just fixing it for them?
13. How would you introduce a junior engineer to a large unfamiliar codebase?
14. Tell me about a time you worked with a distributed, remote, or offshore team. What made it work?

### Quality and ownership
15. Tell me about a production incident you handled. What did you do in the moment, and what changed afterward?
16. Tell me about a time a bug slipped through. What was the root cause and what did you change?
17. Tell me about the project you are proudest of and its measurable impact.
18. Tell me about a time you failed or made a significant mistake.
19. Tell me about a time you had to learn a new technology quickly to deliver something.

## Coaching notes
- **Missing result:** ask "what happened afterward, and did it stick?" Help them find a number or an observable change (time saved, errors reduced, other teams unblocked, same mistake not repeated).
- **Missing trade-off:** ask "what did you give up?"
- **Too much "we":** ask what *they* personally decided or did.
- **Mentoring answers:** a strong one names the person's specific struggle, the teaching approach (they taught the tool and the reasoning, not just the fix; asked a question that led the junior to find the problem), and evidence the lesson was applied independently afterward.
- **Disagreement answers:** strong ones show understanding of the other person's constraint, a decision made on criteria (maintainability, risk, cost), and a resolution that kept the relationship intact. A hybrid solution that combines both views is often a good sign.
- **Failure answers:** honest ownership, the root cause, a concrete process change. Avoid humble-brags.
- **Time limit:** most STAR answers should land in 90 to 150 seconds. Have them tighten if longer.
- **Reuse:** help the candidate build a small set of 4 to 6 strong stories that can be bent to fit different questions (a rebuild, a conflict, a failure, a mentoring moment, a tight deadline, a cross-team win).
